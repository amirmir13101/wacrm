## Cannibalization conclusion

Safe with boundaries.

This article must not try to rank for the broad homepage keyword `whatsapp crm` or replace commercial pages such as:

- `/` — broad WhatsApp CRM positioning
- `/features` — WhatsApp Business API/product overview
- `/features/team-inbox` — team inbox
- `/features/automation` — automation
- `/features/flows` — flows/chatbot
- `/use-cases/sales` — WhatsApp sales

The article should focus on a practical setup framework:

- choosing the right integration route
- official WhatsApp Business Platform/API requirements
- embedded signup / provider setup
- webhooks and message events
- contact mapping
- inbox assignment
- template and opt-in considerations
- automation and handoff
- analytics and testing checklist

It should internally link to the relevant commercial pages where users need product-specific details, but the article itself should answer the setup question comprehensively.

## Content angle for TalkWagon

Most competitor pages explain benefits or list generic setup steps. TalkWagon’s article should be more useful by giving readers a full decision framework:

1. When a CRM integration is actually needed
2. Five integration models: manual Business App, native CRM connector, WhatsApp-native CRM, provider/BSP bridge, and custom API
3. What data should sync: contacts, conversations, opt-in status, tags, pipeline stage, owner, templates, and message status
4. What should not be synced or stored carelessly: passwords, OTPs, full card data, private credentials, and sensitive regulated data
5. How webhooks fit into the flow
6. How teams should assign, route, and hand off conversations
7. How to test the integration before going live
8. Common failure modes: duplicate contacts, broken opt-ins, template mismatch, missing ownership, stale CRM records, and webhook gaps

## Proposed outline

1. What “integrating WhatsApp with CRM” actually means

11. How TalkWagon supports WhatsApp CRM workflows

## Recommended length

Recommended useful article length: approximately 3,200–4,000 words, depending on final competitor gap review during implementation.

The article should be complete enough that a reader does not need to open generic competitor documentation just to understand the setup decisions. Official links can be included as citations/reference points, but the explanation should stand on its own.
